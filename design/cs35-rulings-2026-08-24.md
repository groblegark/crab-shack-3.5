## 4. E4's staff ladder — RUNGS, WITH 6 KEPT

> **Matt:** "Keep 6 as a rung, add higher ones"

His preview of the shape, written before it was built: `HEAD_CAP.steps =
[4, 6, 8, 12]`. A town starts at 6 (today's behaviour) and a vote moves it one
rung, which is what turns the cap from a wall into politics. **That arithmetic

note below, and `game.js`'s `HEAD_CAP` for the ladder itself.

Separately and regardless of E4: the campaign platform refusing more than 6

never voted for. The ladder extends instead, so 0 and 6 keep both their
meaning and their index.

### The same ruling, given directly in the operator session

Measured independently (design/cs35-research/rung-vacuity-2026-08-24.md): 109
crabs in 8 towns over 30 lived days, **not one chose the 6, 8 or 12 rungs**.
94 want no limit at all, 15 want four staff, and every town reads policy cap 0
after 30 days — so the founding six-head cap is voted away at the first
election. The mechanism is not a bug: `capStake100` makes a limit cost the shop
at it, pay the shops that are not, and charge a jobless crab −18 because a cap
is "a posting that never goes up on the board". A structural majority is
against any limit.

Three options were put up: accept it, re-weight `capStake100` so somebody
genuinely wants a cap, or make the founding cap harder to repeal.

> **Matt:** "eh that's interesting enough; we can keep it. not every single
> rule needs to be instantly load bearing in a complex culture, at some point
> extra degrees of freedom have to be allowed"

Ruling: **keep the ladder as built.** The upper rungs stay reachable by the
player's campaign dial and unchosen by the electorate, and the founding cap
stays a thing the town may repeal.

**This is a general principle for the rest of the migration, not a one-off.**
E4, E5 and E7 all add knobs to the crab document, and the standing bar for
this project — a mutation must BITE, a rule must be load-bearing — was about
to be applied to *authored degrees of freedom*, where it does not belong. A
rung nobody currently votes for is not dead code: it is room for a culture, a
future weighting, or a player who argues differently. The vacuity rule governs
INSTRUMENTS — a test that cannot fail, a pin that measures itself — not the
expressive range of the document.

Corollary for whoever writes the E4/E5 scenarios: do not add a scenario that
asserts every declared option gets exercised. Assert that a declared option
CAN be exercised and is refused when malformed.

## 6. What earns a glad departure card — OVERALL CONDITION AT EXIT

Decision `kd-utEb1SjsX8`, following ruling A on `kd-d0eIIm5FJF`. The question
was what a CRAB guest must do to earn `delight`, which never won once in 4,399
cards — not because it is outweighed (it is weight 66 against hungry's 44) but
because its gate `de >= 1` can only be crossed by a NON-CRAB guest eating their
own culture's dish (`game.js:13453`), and `tasteW` returns exactly 1 for a crab
against the `>= 1.5` test behind it. **Two independent locks: a gate change must
clear both or it is a no-op.**

(Corrected at `b3a6b4a`, and it does not weaken the ruling — see `kd-6V9DDm4Cqm`.
`delight` is not "the table's only `glad` rule": four rules are `glad` and two
are `made`, and glad/made together won 66 of the 4,399 cards. The premise that
survives, and the one this ruling actually rests on, is narrower and stronger —
`delight` is the rule **no crab can ever trip**. A rare register is a balance
question; a register a species is locked out of is a structural one.)

> **Matt:** "it should be their overall condition at exit (visitors should have
> sleep/fed/sip etc. just like citizens now) for the moment. Medium term it
> should be derived from a cultural/class/individual weight matrix that also
> influences their decisions. As another note we'd also like for visitors to
> choose when they depart in their neural-net/decision policy"

**This is a bigger answer than the question asked, and the extra scope is the
point.** None of the five options offered were picked. The gate is not a
predicate to be chosen from a list — it is a READOUT of the guest's whole
condition.

**One check on the parenthetical, so nobody builds a prerequisite that already
exists:** visitors DO already carry the citizen need set. `VIS_RATE`
(`game.js:12493`) gives them hunger, thirst, dirt, bored and tired at Q20
per-tick rates, `VIS_WANT` gives the thresholds, and `BODY.R` is that same
table — the physiology is shared, not parallel. What is missing is not the
needs; it is that **nothing reads them at exit**. `delight` gates on the
foodways-only `de` counter instead of on the condition the guest is actually
carrying down the gangway. So the near-term change is a readout over state
that is already there, which makes it considerably smaller than it sounds.

Three horizons, and they must not be collapsed into one change:

1. **NOW** — `delight` reads the guest's overall condition at exit instead of
   the foodways-only `de` counter. The needs already exist (see above); this
   is a readout, plus whatever it takes to make "overall condition" a single
   legible number. Tracked at `kd-ICjpq0dCrb`.
2. **MEDIUM TERM** — the weighting comes from a cultural/class/individual
   weight matrix, and *the same matrix influences their decisions*. That is
   one mechanism serving both the card and the behaviour, not two.
3. **ALSO ASKED, separately** — visitors choose WHEN they depart, inside their
   neural-net/decision policy. This is a decision-policy change, not a
   departure-card change; it belongs with the neural-crab work
   (`kd-Xpj0w3m90C`), not with the card.

Note what (2) implies for anyone building (1): the near-term readout should be
shaped so a weight matrix can replace its coefficients later without
re-litigating the gate. Do not hard-code a bespoke condition formula that the
matrix will have to fight.
