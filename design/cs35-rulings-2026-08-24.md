## 4. E4's staff ladder — RUNGS, WITH 6 KEPT

> **Matt:** "Keep 6 as a rung, add higher ones"

His preview of the shape, written before it was built: `HEAD_CAP.steps =
[4, 6, 8, 12]`. A town starts at 6 (today's behaviour) and a vote moves it one
rung, which is what turns the cap from a wall into politics. **That arithmetic
is the preview being responded to, not what shipped** — see the implementation
note below, and `game.js`'s `HEAD_CAP` for the ladder itself.

Separately and regardless of E4: the campaign platform refusing more than 6
staff, and its window text running off the screen, are UI defects. They are
not blocked on this ruling.

**Implemented as `[0, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12]`, not the previewed `[4, 6, 8, 12]`.**
The preview would have deleted step 0, and step 0 is NO LIMIT — the founding
policy every pre-feature save loads as. Replacing the ladder would have
silently converted every existing town's "no limit" into a four-head cap it
never voted for. The ladder extends instead, so 0 and 6 keep both their
meaning and their index.

## 5. The town repeals its own house limit — WORKING AS DESIGNED

**RULED TWICE, THROUGH TWO CHANNELS, IN AGREEMENT.** Matt answered this
question both on decision kd-Xqri0Ws081 (filed by a cs pod) and directly in
the operator session, independently and within the same hour. Both quotes are
genuine and both are recorded below; neither supersedes the other. The
duplication is a process note worth keeping: the same measurement was run
twice — a pod's `e4-rung-reachability.md` and the operator's
`rung-vacuity-2026-08-24.md`, the pod using `tools/rungprobe.mjs` that the
operator had committed — because work proceeded in two places at once. The
numbers agree exactly (`{0: 94, 3: 15}`, policyCap 0 in all eight towns),
which is a real cross-check, but it was not a free one.

> **Matt:** option D — "Working as designed. Only shop owners want a hiring
> cap; a town voting its limit away is the ballot doing its job. Record it in
> the rulings and stop treating the repeal as a smell."

Ruled on decision kd-Xqri0Ws081, against measurement rather than argument:
8 towns x 30 lived days, 109 voters, chosen cap index `{0: 94, 3: 15}` and
`policyCap: 0` in all eight towns by day 30. Full write-up and the mutation
demo that proved the mechanism: `design/cs35-research/e4-rung-reachability.md`.

What is now settled, and must not be re-opened as a bug:

- **The repeal is the ballot working.** A town founded at the 6-head cap voting
  it away within a month is drama the election exists to produce, not a defect.
- **The electorate for this policy is shop owners.** `capStake100` sits entirely
  inside `if (c.p.owner)` and that is the intended shape: a crab with no till
  has no stake in a hiring limit. Wage earners scoring the ladder flat-zero, and
  `capAsk`'s -1 handing every non-binding rung to NO LIMIT, are **not** bugs to
  be fixed.
- **The 8- and 12-rungs stay** even though no NPC ever votes for them. They are
  a player affordance — the campaign dial Matt asked for from play — and the
  ladder is inert on the floor by construction. Ruling 4 stands unchanged.

Two consequences that bind future work:

1. **E4 slice 2 (kd-Ah5oIRS3QC) transcribes `capStake100` AS-IS.** No
   reweighting, no widening, no special-casing to make the upper rungs
   reachable. The decision was taken *before* the transcription precisely so it
   would be transcribed once, and correctly.
2. **The founding-cap instruction means less than it sounds like, knowingly.**
   "The starting condition should be 6 staff" is honoured at founding and the
   town is free to vote it away. That gap was named in the decision context and
   accepted, not overlooked.

Reversed by this ruling: the write-up's own closing framing ("a design question
for Matt, not an agent's call ... not ruled at all") and the `RULING NEEDED`
task kd-sz2YvajA2d, whose option (a) is what Matt chose. `capStake100`
re-weighting — the write-up's option B and that task's option (b) — is
**declined**, not deferred.

### The same ruling, given directly in the operator session

Measured independently (design/cs35-research/rung-vacuity-2026-08-24.md): 109
crabs in 8 towns over 30 lived days, **not one chose the 6, 8 or 12 rungs**.
94 want no limit at all, 15 want four staff, and every town reads policy cap 0
after 30 days — so the founding six-head cap is voted away at the first
election. The mechanism is not a bug: `capStake100` makes a limit cost the shop
at it, pay the shops that are not, and charge a jobless crab −18 because a cap
is "a posting that never goes up on the board". A structural majority is
against any limit.

Three options were put up: accept it, re-weight `capStake100` so somebody
genuinely wants a cap, or make the founding cap harder to repeal.

> **Matt:** "eh that's interesting enough; we can keep it. not every single
> rule needs to be instantly load bearing in a complex culture, at some point
> extra degrees of freedom have to be allowed"

Ruling: **keep the ladder as built.** The upper rungs stay reachable by the
player's campaign dial and unchosen by the electorate, and the founding cap
stays a thing the town may repeal.

**This is a general principle for the rest of the migration, not a one-off.**
E4, E5 and E7 all add knobs to the crab document, and the standing bar for
this project — a mutation must BITE, a rule must be load-bearing — was about
to be applied to *authored degrees of freedom*, where it does not belong. A
rung nobody currently votes for is not dead code: it is room for a culture, a
future weighting, or a player who argues differently. The vacuity rule governs
INSTRUMENTS — a test that cannot fail, a pin that measures itself — not the
expressive range of the document.

Corollary for whoever writes the E4/E5 scenarios: do not add a scenario that
asserts every declared option gets exercised. Assert that a declared option
CAN be exercised and is refused when malformed.
